# Constant Slow Pursuit — native SEO/AEO/GEO research

Date: 2026-09-20  
Route: `/drills/visual-tracking/constant-slow-pursuit`  
Scope: one drill and six localized markets. No IndexNow, Bing submission, or deployment push was performed.

## Search intent and drill fit

This drill moves one target slowly around a continuous Lissajous path and lets the player tune duration, target size, speed, visibility, trail, and contrast. The best-fit intent is a free browser smooth-pursuit / dynamic-vision exercise for visual attention, gaze-following practice, and gaming warm-up. It is not an eye examination and does not diagnose a vision or neurological condition.

## SERP / feature gap findings

- Native results tend to describe the action—following a moving point, target, or object—rather than a literal translated product name. The page therefore uses action-led titles and short subtitles.
- Existing pages commonly discuss therapy, sports vision, or apps. SkillDrills can occupy the free browser practice niche with immediate play, adjustable speed, a continuous path, and transparent device limitations.
- AEO copy will define smooth pursuit, catch-up saccades, target velocity, and gaze stability in direct answer blocks without claiming that a cursor score records eye movements.
- The interactive start card exposes duration, speed, target size, color, path visibility, random speed, trail, glow, scanlines, and day mode. Those controls become searchable, localized utility rather than hidden settings.
- The guide will explain ergonomics and visual comfort. Users should stop if tracking causes pain, dizziness, nausea, or persistent blur and seek appropriate professional advice for symptoms.

## Data limitations

- The local Bing keyword client was attempted for all six country/language pairs and was blocked by Windows socket policy (`WinError 10013`) before returning volume rows. No Bing volume number is claimed.
- Google Trends query pages were not accessible through the web retrieval tool in this run; no regional trend index is fabricated.
- Search Console access was not available in this run. Native live pages were used for phrasing, intent, and feature review, not search-volume evidence.
- Autocomplete is not treated as monthly volume, and no numeric low-competition or #1-ranking probability is asserted.
- Every locale receives independently authored UI, metadata, guide, and FAQ copy. No English page is translated into a target language.

## Implementation decisions

1. Lead markets with native intent: `동체시력 훈련`, `動体視力 トレーニング`, `Blickfolgeübung`, `exercício de perseguição visual`, `ejercicio de seguimiento suave`, and `exercice de poursuite visuelle`.
2. Keep titles and subtitles short; put the broader semantic cluster in the guide, settings, protocols, and FAQs.
3. Preserve the actual mechanics: continuous Lissajous path, low-velocity pursuit, optional random acceleration, target size, path visibility, trail, contrast, and session duration.
4. Generate locale-specific metadata, canonical/alternate links, breadcrumbs, FAQPage, HowTo, SoftwareApplication, WebApplication, and VideoGame schema.
5. Avoid medical claims and distinguish eye-movement concepts from pointer-based browser performance.
